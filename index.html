What can a machine know, feel and judge?

Themis is an independent, student-led AI research lab. We use neuroscience to answer the philosophical questions of AI.

Can a machine infer morality?

Judging right from wrong takes more than rules. We look at how the brain weighs harm, fairness and intent, and ask what it would take to model that.

Can a machine have emotion?

Emotion is a biological process before it is a feeling. We study the circuits behind it to see what, if anything, could be reproduced in software.

Can a machine be conscious?

Nobody has settled what consciousness is, and Themis does not claim an answer. We test small, specific ideas from neuroscience and report what holds up.

How we work

A small, repeatable loop that keeps every claim tied to evidence.

  1. 01

    Study the brain

    We read the neuroscience on cortical circuits, synapses and neural dynamics, and pick out mechanisms that could matter for machine intelligence.

  2. 02

    Build small models

    Ideas become small, testable models that run on modest hardware.

  3. 03

    Test against data

    Models are compared with public brain-imaging datasets, from healthy and dysfunctional brains, to see where cognition holds and where it fails.

  4. 04

    Publish openly

    Methods, code and results are shared, including the failures.

Where the answers come from

Themis draws on three fields and keeps them in conversation.

Neuroscience

Cortical circuits, synapses, and how they give rise to thought.

The brain

Healthy and dysfunctional brain data from public research datasets, to see where cognition breaks.

Philosophy and science

Work on mind, ethics and consciousness, to keep the questions honest.

Principles

How Themis chooses what to work on, and what to claim.

Independent

Themis belongs to no company or institution. Questions are chosen by curiosity, not by a product roadmap.

Student-led

Themis is run by a final-year high school student, and says so.

Honest about scale

Resources are limited: public data, open-source tools and small-scale compute. Claims stay within what the work can support.

Philosophy included

Questions about morality, emotion and consciousness need philosophy as much as engineering, so both are part of the method.

Where things stand

Themis is at an early stage. The work right now is reading, small experiments, and building a public record of both. Research notes will appear on Instagram and GitHub as they are completed.

The name comes from Themis, the Greek Titaness of divine order and law.
